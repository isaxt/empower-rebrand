Overview

A ride belongs to both people in it.

A brand and product redesign for Empower, a rideshare network built around agency, not convenience.

Type
Brand identity, campaign and app redesign
Role
Brand strategy, visual identity, campaign design, product design
Tools
Figma

Context and problem

Empower is a rideshare network where drivers set their own rates and keep 100% of the fare, and riders choose drivers they trust. The mission was real. The brand and the app were not yet saying so: the website made big promises, and the app’s interface didn’t visibly deliver on them.

Case study by Isabella Tedesco. Third-party marks shown in mockups belong to their owners.

Decision 01
Show the drivers, then the price. The old flow asked for a destination and returned a car. The redesign opens on the people: trusted drivers nearby, favorites marked, and a ride list that pairs each driver with their own fare and a local range for the trip.
Decision 02
Same functions, one voice. Account, help and credits already existed. The work was making them sound and look like the brand: serif headlines for orientation, quiet Inter for utility, one blue instead of three.
Decision 03
The driver sets the rate. On the driver side the rate is a first-class screen, with a suggested rate and nearby drivers for context. Earnings close the loop and end on a single number: 100% you keep.
Applications
Next to the incumbent, on the same glass. A gradient pill sits beside Uber’s sticker on a windshield, and shows up again on a rear window.
Applications
On walls and platforms. Campaign lines make the promise in a few words: “Keep 100% of Every Fare” in London, “A Ride Goes Both Ways” on a transit platform.
Applications
From the glass of a car to the glass of a phone. The same pill, the same two voices.
Outcome
One idea, from billboard to button. The result is a brand that says the same thing everywhere it appears: on a windshield, on a wall, and on the screen in a rider’s hand. The campaign lines and the interface make the same promise, and the interface now backs it up.
